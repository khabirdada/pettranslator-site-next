This tag collects the articles that answer the dog behavior questions owners type into Google in moments of genuine curiosity or worry — "Why does my dog stare at me?", "Why is my dog following me everywhere?", "Why does my dog yawn when not tired?", "Why is my dog pacing at night?" Each one gets a behaviorist's answer rather than the recycled pet-blog version.

What each article gives you: a TL;DR with the most likely answer in 60 words; a breakdown of the most common contexts behind the behavior; the dominance-theory misreads to discard; an action plan grounded in force-free, AVSAB-aligned methodology; and explicit guidance on when the behavior crosses the line into a vet or behaviorist visit.

The articles share a hard rule: when a behavior change happens in a previously stable dog (especially a senior dog), the first call is a vet, not a trainer. Medical issues mimic behavior issues constantly — pain, cognitive decline, vision loss, hyperthyroidism, hormone changes. Reading what your dog is communicating is the second step. The first step is ruling out a physical cause.
