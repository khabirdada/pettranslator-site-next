Most pet behavior questions get answered on the internet with folk wisdom, dominance-theory holdovers, or "trust me, I've had dogs my whole life" energy. That's not what's here. Every article in this tag is written against the same discipline: describe the visible behavior first, cite the peer-reviewed literature where it exists, distinguish what's actually known from what's still being studied, and give the owner something they can do today without harming the animal.

The reason this tag exists as a separate collection — instead of blending into training or medical categories — is that "why does my dog do that?" is a distinct genre of question. It's not usually a training problem or a medical problem. It's a communication gap between two species that evolved for very different social lives. Reading a dog's whale eye, or a cat's tail flick, or a puppy's lip lick is a learnable skill, and it's the skill that lets an owner intervene before a behavior becomes a bite. Some articles here focus on single body-language signals; others on ambiguous situations owners describe to their vet ("she started peeing on the couch"); others on the emotional states — anxiety, frustration, overstimulation, appeasement — that produce those behaviors.

The throughline: the animal is always making sense on its own terms. Our job as owners is to become fluent enough to hear it. Where a question has a straightforward answer, we give it. Where the literature disagrees, we say so. Where the honest answer is "see your vet," we don't try to solve it here.
