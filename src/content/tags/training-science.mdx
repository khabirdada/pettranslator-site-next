Modern animal training is a science with a citation trail. The core mechanisms — operant conditioning, classical conditioning, differential reinforcement, extinction — have been experimentally validated across species for the better part of a century. The applied practice for companion animals now has professional accrediting bodies (KPA, IAABC, CCPDT, AVSAB) with published standards for what constitutes ethical, effective training. Every article in this tag maps to that literature and to the LIMA (Least Intrusive, Minimally Aversive) principle that AVSAB, the American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior, has adopted as the professional standard.

Two things you will not find here. First, dominance-theory-based methods. The "alpha" and "pack leader" frameworks derived from David Mech's 1970 wolf-pack observations were formally retracted by Mech himself decades ago after he studied wild wolves and discovered his captive-wolf model was an artifact of confinement. Applied to dogs, dominance theory is both scientifically wrong and welfare-harmful — it produces the exact fear-based aggression it claims to prevent. Second, any endorsement of aversive tools (prong collars, e-collars, "balanced" training). The peer-reviewed evidence on aversives is unambiguous: they suppress behavior without teaching alternatives, elevate stress markers, and increase the probability of redirected aggression. The AVSAB position statement, the American Animal Hospital Association's guidelines, and the European Society of Veterinary Clinical Ethology all converge on this point.

What you will find: articles on how positive reinforcement actually works at the neurological level; when to use classical vs. operant conditioning; how socialization windows shape adult behavior; what the research says about crate training, recall, loose-leash walking, and the training problems every owner actually has. If your favorite trainer disagrees with any of it, ask them for their citations. Then read what we've cited. The animal-behavior literature is public. This tag is a curated entry point.
