## Decision

The queue now contains more than a month of distinct editorial work without pretending that every low-KD term is easy. The strongest immediate openings are narrow behaviors where the current result page still relies on one exact article plus community discussion. Large head terms such as `why do cats hiss`, `why do dogs roll in the grass`, and `what does it mean when a cat purrs` have attractive Semrush numbers but strong exact-intent competition; they belong in reserve, not at the front of the schedule. Cats.com's behavior folder produced only 126 ranking terms and was dominated by broad, ambiguous, below-threshold, or medically misleading queries, so no Cats.com row survived the raw editorial filter.

This is a rolling research and production queue. It does not override the indexing-backlog gate. Before any item becomes a new canonical URL, refresh its live top 10, collect audience evidence, verify claims with authoritative sources, confirm a distinct intent, identify useful internal-link donors, and run the full content SOP.

## Safety, medical, or off-intent holding area

Do not schedule these as ordinary behavior posts without qualified review and a strong reader-safety reason: dogs or puppies eating poop, dirt, grass compulsively, sticks, tampons, urine, or other non-food objects; dogs licking or drinking urine; cats pooping outside the litter box; dogs urinating on other dogs; puppy rage syndrome; dog-bite intervention; and broad symptom terms such as excessive panting. The raw demand is real, but the current PetTranslator product and editorial model do not justify casual coverage.

## Daily operating cadence

1. Keep at least 30 distinct actionable items across the front, reserve, and refresh queues. Replenish weekly from new behavior-focused competitor portfolios before the count falls below 30.
2. Select one intent per working day. During an indexing backlog, prefer refreshes, consolidation, internal links, and research briefs over new canonical URLs.
3. Recheck the current US top 10 on the day the brief starts. A Semrush score is only a discovery signal.
4. Complete the SOP research brief: audience and intent, competitor coverage, audience evidence, authoritative verification, unique value, cannibalization, and at least four natural inbound-link donors where they exist.
5. Draft and publish only after the backlog gate and all content checks pass. Verify the production sitemap, request indexing, and record the page's monitoring date after publication.
