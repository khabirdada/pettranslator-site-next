Urgent informational and safety intent. The reader has seen a cat attack a nearby person or pet after becoming aroused by something else and needs to prevent injury, understand the event and reunite household cats safely.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from Cornell, ASPCA, VCA, PetMD and veterinary reviews define redirected aggression as aggression toward an accessible target when the original arousing stimulus cannot be reached. Common examples are outdoor cats, loud noises and household conflict. Strong sources warn against approaching or handling a highly aroused cat and note that arousal can persist. Multi-cat guidance adds that a victim may become fearful, so conflict can continue after the original trigger disappears.

## Content opportunity

- Lead with an immediate no-hands safety sequence.

- Explain safe separation without grabbing, chasing or punishment.

- Cover window management and humane outdoor-cat deterrence.
