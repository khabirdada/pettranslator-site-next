## Decision

Publish a dedicated guide at `/blog/pet-stress-tracker`.

The neighboring journal page explains how to log behavior; the stress-tracker page explains what each signal can and cannot measure, how to build an individual baseline and how to respond without turning a score into a diagnosis.

## Current result pattern

Results include AI photo/video apps promising mood or calmness scores, smart collars using activity and physiological measures, GPS/activity products, cat-specific stress scores and general care logs. Commercial claims often blur arousal, stress, anxiety, emotion and disease. The defensible opportunity is a comparison framework based on individual baseline, context, multiple evidence streams, transparent uncertainty and medical referral.

## Evidence

- AAHA lists nonspecific distress signs across posture, movement, vocalization, elimination, appetite, grooming, social contact and reactivity.
- Reviews of canine welfare measurement find HR/HRV can reflect arousal of positive or negative valence and should be combined with behavior/context.
- Cortisol varies with method, timing, activity and health; it is not a simple consumer point score.
- Reviews of the Cat Stress Score report usefulness in defined settings but observer training, omitted behaviors, brief observation and context limit generalization.
- Sudden behavior change can reflect pain or disease and needs veterinary assessment.

## Product boundary

PetTranslator analyzes submitted media and stores structured observations/trends on paid plans. It is not continuous monitoring, a validated clinical stress scale, a wearable, a cortisol test or a diagnostic device. Position its advantage as transparent visible markers plus context and confidence, not direct measurement of subjective emotion.
