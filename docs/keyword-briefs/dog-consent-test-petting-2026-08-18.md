# Keyword brief: dog consent test petting

## Search intent

Informational with bite-prevention and relationship intent. The reader wants a quick way to tell whether a dog wants petting to begin or continue.

## Duplication audit

`/blog/why-does-my-dog-show-belly` contains a short start-stop test specific to belly exposure. Several stress and freezing guides say to stop unwanted touch, but no page owns the general petting decision across familiar dogs, unfamiliar dogs, children, ambiguous signals and possible pain.

Create a dedicated guide. Keep the belly article focused on interpreting that posture and replace its overconfident test claims with a concise link to this page.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from Preventive Vet, welfare organizations, foster manuals and trainer resources commonly recommend about three seconds of touch followed by a pause. Most divide the response into “continue” or “stop.” Veterinary guidance adds the more important context: petting is not rewarding to every dog, a relaxed body matters, and tension, avoidance or resistance during handling should not be forced.

The search phrase is established in public education, but the three-second protocol is not a validated diagnostic test. Research shows that many dogs seek or prefer petting in particular experiments; it does not establish that every approach, lean or paw movement requests a specific kind of touch.

## Content opportunity

- Define the consent test as a brief touch-pause-observe check, not a pass/fail diagnosis.
- Explain observable assent and withdrawal while retaining the familiar search term.
- Start only when the dog is loose, awake, free to leave and choosing proximity.
- Compare clear re-engagement, clear disengagement and ambiguous responses.
- Warn that proximity, taking food, a wag or remaining still does not independently prove comfort.
- Cover safer touch locations and why reaching over the head, hugging or cornering changes the test.
- Give stricter rules for children and unfamiliar dogs.
- Separate optional affection from essential veterinary or grooming care.
- Flag new touch sensitivity, location-specific reactions and behavior change for veterinary assessment.
