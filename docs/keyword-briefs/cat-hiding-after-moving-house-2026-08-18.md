# Keyword brief: cat hiding after moving house

## Decision

Publish a dedicated guide at `/blog/cat-hiding-after-moving-house`.

The existing `/blog/cat-hiding-under-the-bed` explains hiding generally and provides medical red flags. It mentions moving only as one possible stressor. The move-specific intent requires a safe-room setup, recovery checkpoints, territory expansion, familiar scent and routine, escape prevention, and outdoor-access planning. The new page should link to the general diagnostic guide rather than repeat it.

## Search intent

- Learn whether hiding after a move is normal and how long it can last.
- Help a cat settle without forcing contact.
- Know whether to use one room or the whole home.
- Decide when the cat is ready to explore more space.
- Recognize appetite, urinary and illness warning signs.
- Prevent escape from an unfamiliar home.

## Target terms

- Primary: `cat hiding after moving house`
- Secondary: `cat hiding after moving`, `cat scared after moving`, `how long will cat hide after moving`, `cat adjusting to new home`, `cat safe room after moving`

## Current result pattern

Current leading results from established cat-welfare organizations consistently recommend:

- a quiet, closed safe room before and after the move
- familiar bedding and scent-bearing items
- food, water, litter, scratcher, carrier and hiding options in the room
- no forced removal from a carrier or hiding place
- expansion at the cat's pace rather than on a fixed timetable
- closed exterior doors, windows and cat flaps during settling
- updated microchip/contact details and registration with a local vet

## Differentiation and safety

- Do not promote the informal 3-3-3 rule as a clinical timetable.
- Do not promise a standard number of days. Use behavior and resource-use checkpoints.
- Avoid pulling the cat out, blocking all hiding, flooding with the whole house, or punishing elimination.
- Separate normal hiding from not eating, straining to urinate, vomiting, breathing difficulty or deterioration.
- Make outdoor timing location-neutral and advise local veterinary/welfare guidance instead of one universal number.
