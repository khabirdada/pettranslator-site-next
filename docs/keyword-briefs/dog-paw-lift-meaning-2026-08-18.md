# Keyword brief: dog paw lift meaning

## Search intent

Informational with a health differential. The reader sees a dog hold one front paw off the ground and wants to know whether it means uncertainty, pointing, anticipation, a learned request or pain.

## Duplication audit

No dedicated paw-lift page exists. `/blog/dog-calming-signals` includes one row and an illustration, while `/blog/dog-displacement-behaviors` lists paw lifting as one possible out-of-context action. The new page should own the differential interpretation and link back from the calming-signals table.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results include AKC on pointing, veterinary body-language guides from Texas A&M and VCA, a specialist calming-signal page, Animal Health Foundation, and peer-reviewed stress studies that operationalize paw lifting. Results commonly assign uncertainty or pointing but rarely cover learned requests and medical triage together.

## Content opportunity

- Separate a held paw lift from pawing at a person, waving and lifting a hind leg to urinate.
- Use trigger, weight distribution, gaze, muscle tone, duration and walking afterward.
- Explain uncertainty as one possible context, not a translation.
- Cover pointing in pointing breeds and non-pointing dogs without assuming ancestry.
- Explain how people can reinforce a paw lift into a request.
- Give a practical paw and gait check without encouraging painful manipulation.
- State that stress ethograms count paw lifting but one occurrence cannot quantify stress.
- Include same-day and urgent veterinary red flags.
