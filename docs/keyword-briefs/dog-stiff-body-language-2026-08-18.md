# Keyword brief: dog stiff body language

## Search intent

Informational with safety and health triage. The reader notices a dog becoming rigid or moving stiffly and wants to distinguish social tension, fear, intense focus, play interruption and physical pain.

## Duplication audit

`/blog/dog-freezing-body-language` owns the transition into complete stillness. `/blog/dog-body-language` briefly covers posture and weight. No page explains stiffness that occurs while a dog remains standing, walking, wagging or playing, or separates behavioral tension from mobility stiffness.

Create a distinct page focused on loss of fluid movement. Add a contextual link from the posture section of the broad body-language guide.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results are broad pages from PetMD, ASPCApro, VCA, AKC, SF SPCA and Best Friends. They contrast loose play with stiff fear or aggression. Pain guidelines and chronic-pain research add altered posture, gait stiffness and reduced activity. Few pages put the behavioral and medical differentials in one decision framework.

## Content opportunity

- Define stiffness as reduced fluidity, not a diagnosis or synonym for aggression.
- Separate stiff-and-moving from a full freeze.
- Use direction of weight, gaze, mouth, tail and trigger to distinguish fear and forward focus.
- Explain that a stiff wag is arousal, not friendliness.
- Compare loose play with play becoming rigid, one-sided or hard to interrupt.
- Cover stiffness after rest, during movement or touch as a pain signal.
- Give safe actions for greetings, play, guarding and handling.
- Include urgent veterinary red flags and video guidance.
