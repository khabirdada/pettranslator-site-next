# Keyword brief: cat stress signs

## Search intent

Informational with medical triage. The reader wants to recognize stress in a cat through body language and longer-term changes in hiding, grooming, appetite, play, social behavior and litter-box use, then decide what to change and when to call a veterinarian.

## Duplication audit

No dedicated cat-stress guide exists. `/blog/cat-body-language` covers momentary ears, eyes, whiskers, tail and posture but not chronic routine changes or an environmental assessment. `/blog/why-is-my-cat-acting-weird` covers broad medical differentials. `/blog/cat-overstimulation-signs` is touch-specific.

The new page should own acute-versus-chronic stress recognition, routine tracking, medical overlap and the five-pillar home audit. Add a contextual link from the low-grade stress cluster in the broad body-language guide.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results include PetMD body-language and stress-list pages, a peer-reviewed veterinary review of physical and emotional health, PDSA, AAFP behavior guidance, a scientific review of olfactory and environmental strategies, Cats Protection resources and several new niche pages. Common signs include hiding, hypervigilance, altered social contact, appetite and grooming changes, house soiling, spraying, scratching, irritability and reduced play.

## Content opportunity

- Separate acute body-language changes from chronic routine changes.
- State that stress, pain and disease signs overlap and are not mutually exclusive.
- Avoid “cats hide illness because predators” as a complete explanation; focus on observed subtlety and baseline change.
- Explain that house soiling and overgrooming need medical workups before a stress-only label.
- Use the AAFP/ISFM five environmental-needs pillars as a practical home audit.
- Address quiet conflict and resource blocking in multi-cat homes.
- Provide a seven-day baseline log and improvement measures.
- Include urgent red flags, especially open-mouth breathing, urinary obstruction signs and prolonged food refusal.
