# Keyword brief: dog hard stare meaning

## Search intent

Informational with immediate safety intent. The reader wants to know whether a fixed, unblinking dog stare is a warning, guarding, predatory focus, attention seeking or a medical change, and what to do without escalating it.

## Duplication audit and decision

`/blog/why-is-my-dog-staring-at-me` already owns the broad staring query, distinguishes soft gaze, requests, guarding and medical change, and explicitly includes “hard stare.” A separate URL would overlap its title, examples, intent and internal-link targets.

Consolidate into the existing canonical page. Expand the hard-versus-soft diagnostic, add social-warning versus guarding versus predatory focus, correct deterministic statements about whale eye and bite sequences, refresh metadata and add an original four-context illustration.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from PetMD, AKC, AVSAB, behavior organizations and resource-guarding guides describe hard gaze with stiffness, closed mouth, lowered head, whale eye or guarding context as a warning. Most search results focus almost entirely on threat and under-explain predatory visual focus, learned request staring and medical blank staring.

## Content opportunity

- Define “hard” through the entire face and body, not seconds of eye contact alone.
- Contrast soft social gaze, learned request, social warning, guarding and predatory focus.
- Explain that reduced blinking is not a timer and hard stare does not guarantee a bite.
- Give a no-touch, no-staring safety response.
- Distinguish a warning directed at someone from blank or unresponsive medical staring.
- Include children, unfamiliar dogs and multi-dog resource safety.
