# Research brief: cat overstimulation signs

**Primary keyword:** cat overstimulation signs

**Intent:** Recognize the early body-language changes that can precede a bite or scratch during petting, play, handling, or a busy environment, then respond safely.

**Reader outcome:** Stop interactions before escalation, distinguish overstimulation from pain and other aggression patterns, and know when veterinary or behavior help is needed.

## Merged topics from the result set

- Definition and common triggers
- Tail swishing or twitching
- Skin rippling
- Ear rotation or flattening
- Dilated pupils
- Tension, freezing and restlessness
- Watching or turning toward the hand
- Growling, swatting, scratching and biting
- Stopping touch and allowing recovery
- Shorter, cat-led petting sessions
- Enrichment and play
- Pain, arthritis, dental disease and feline hyperesthesia as differentials

## Gaps this article should fill

- Separate early, middle and late signs instead of presenting an unordered list.
- Explain that purring or soliciting contact does not guarantee continued consent to touch.
- Give a practical pause test that lets the cat choose whether contact resumes.
- Separate petting overstimulation, intense play, redirected arousal and pain-related aggression.
- Warn against unsupported claims that overstimulation is always a painful neurological reaction.
- Explain what a photo misses and why short video can show the sequence better.
- Avoid promising that tolerance will always increase with training.
