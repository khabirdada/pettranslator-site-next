# Keyword brief: pet behavior photo analysis

## Decision

Publish a species-neutral parent guide at `/blog/pet-behavior-photo-analysis`.

The page should sit above `/blog/ai-dog-body-language-analyzer` and `/blog/ai-cat-body-language-analyzer`. It should not repeat their signal inventories. Its distinct jobs are explaining what one photo can preserve, how to select or capture useful evidence, when video is required, how to write context, and how to evaluate photo-analysis claims.

## Current result pattern

Current results are mainly commercial upload tools. Common features are photo/video input, mood or behavior labels, confidence and recommendations. Frequent overclaims include exact mind reading, unexplained accuracy, breed and health screening from the same image, and universal advice. PetTranslator already appears in the result set, so this page should strengthen relevance while making more restrained claims than competitors.

## Target terms

- Primary: `pet behavior photo analysis`
- Secondary: `analyze pet behavior from photo`, `pet photo behavior analyzer`, `AI pet photo analysis`, `dog and cat behavior photo analysis`

## Search intent

- Upload a pet photo for a behavior interpretation.
- Learn which photo to choose.
- Understand what a still image can and cannot reveal.
- Compare dog and cat analysis needs.
- Receive practical next steps without a novelty caption.

## Content boundaries

- Dogs and cats are the supported species; do not imply other animals are supported.
- Treat the page as a capture-and-evidence hub, not a third body-language encyclopedia.
- Route canine and feline readers to their dedicated analyzer guides.
- Separate observable markers, interpretation, confidence and action.
- Do not claim emotion classification accuracy, health screening, diagnosis or bite prediction.

## Evidence base

- Canine research shows whole-body attention can improve human emotion categorization and that context affects interpretation.
- Feline research shows people find relaxed, tense and fearful states difficult to distinguish even from clear full-body video.
- Cross-modal research supports video and contextual information when a still frame is insufficient.
- AVSAB and AAFP/ISFM guidance support humane, choice-preserving responses.
