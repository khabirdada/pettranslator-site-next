# Keyword brief: dog appeasement gestures

## Search intent

Informational. The reader wants a list of canine appeasement signals, what they mean, how they differ from fear or friendly greeting and how a person should respond.

## Duplication audit and decision

`/blog/dog-calming-signals` already owns the same action set, intent and primary research: head turns, nose licking, body turns, low posture, paw lifts, sniffing, slowing and belly exposure. A separate “dog appeasement gestures” URL would repeat the list and compete for the same queries.

Consolidate into `/blog/dog-calming-signals`. Add the exact vocabulary, distinguish active affiliative appeasement from passive fear-related appeasement, explain why “submissive” and “guilty” are poor shortcuts, add a purpose-built illustration and strengthen FAQs. Retain the existing original hero because it accurately represents the shared topic.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from PetMD, AKC, VCA, Dogs Trust, Whole Dog Journal and trainer sites list lowered posture, ears back, lip licking, muzzle licking, paw lift, rolling over, soft squint and sometimes urination. Primary studies from Firnkes et al., Mariti et al. and Cavalli et al. examine candidate appeasement or calming signals but do not support translating each gesture in isolation.

## Content opportunity

- Define appeasement as a possible social function, not a fixed visual code.
- Contrast voluntary approach with loose affiliative gestures against low, still, retreat-oriented fear.
- Explain that active appeasement does not automatically invite touch.
- Separate appeasement from displacement by description versus inferred function.
- Correct guilt, submission and dominance framing.
- Provide a response based on movement, tension, exit access and recovery.
