# Keyword brief: cat petting aggression

## Search intent

Informational and problem-solving. The reader's cat solicits or accepts petting, then becomes tense, swats, scratches or bites. They want causes, warning signs, a safer petting method and guidance on pain or professional help.

## Current top-result patterns

The current results include Cornell, ASPCA, a peer-reviewed review of owner-directed feline aggression, a controlled human-cat interaction study, BC SPCA, AAFP/ISFM handling guidelines, VCA, Merck and PetMD. Common coverage includes tail lashing, ears back, pupil change, petting duration, preferred head/neck touch, punishment avoidance and pain screening.

## Content opportunity

- State that “petting-induced aggression” is a descriptive label, not proof of one cause.
- Separate repetitive touch, loss of control, fear/handling history, play, redirected arousal and pain.
- Use the evidence-backed CAT framework: Choice and control, Attention, Touch location.
- Explain the study outcome without implying every cat prefers identical touch.
- Keep the pause test at 3 to 5 seconds and let the cat reinitiate.
- Flag that “up to 40%” applies to aggression cases seen by referral services, not all cats.
- Reject unsupported neurological or static-electricity explanations as established mechanisms.
