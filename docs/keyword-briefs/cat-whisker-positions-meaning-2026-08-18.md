# Keyword brief: cat whisker positions meaning

## Search intent

Informational. The reader wants a visual guide to forward, neutral and pulled-back cat whiskers, plus help separating curiosity, fear, aggression and pain.

## Duplication audit

The existing `/blog/cat-body-language` page has a short whisker section, but there is no dedicated whisker-position guide. This article should become the detailed supporting page. Add one contextual link from the broad body-language guide and avoid repeating its full ears, eyes and tail coverage.

## Current result-page patterns

The leading pages cover whisker anatomy, neutral whiskers, forward whiskers, whiskers drawn toward the cheeks, hunting, aggression, pain and whisker care. Most present positions as a simple mood dictionary. Several conflict about whether forward whiskers mean curiosity, threat or pain.

## Content opportunity

- Explain that forward means active attention, not automatically friendliness.
- Teach readers to compare with the individual cat's neutral baseline.
- Separate observable position from interpretation.
- Explain why ears, eyes, posture, tail and immediate context change the reading.
- Cover the Feline Grimace Scale accurately: whisker change is one of five action units, not a stand-alone pain test.
- Note that static frontal photos make whisker position difficult to score.
- Treat whisker-fatigue claims cautiously because controlled evidence is limited.
- Give practical photo and video tips plus clear veterinary escalation signs.
