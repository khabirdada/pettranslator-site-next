# Keyword brief: cat crouching body language

## Search intent

Informational with medical triage. The reader sees a cat holding the body low or compact and wants to distinguish normal loafing or stalking from fear, defensive arousal, pain and litter-box straining.

## Duplication audit

`/blog/cat-body-language` gives crouching two sentences. `/blog/cat-stress-signs` notes that stress crouching can resemble hunting or loafing. `/blog/why-is-my-cat-acting-weird` flags urinary straining. No article compares the postures using weight, muscle tension, head, face, tail, trigger, movement and duration, or brings behavioral and medical decisions into one framework.

Create a dedicated differential guide. Link to the broad body-language page and medical pages rather than repeating their complete scope.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from PetMD, VCA, ASPCA, Cats Protection and general pet sites emphasize fearful crouching, stalking and pain. ISFM acute-pain guidelines, AAHA/AAFP pain guidance and feline acute-pain ethograms identify crouched or hunched posture as one possible pain sign alongside lowered head, squinting, reduced activity and altered interaction. Search results rarely give urinary straining enough prominence or distinguish a soft loaf from a tense crouch precisely.

## Content opportunity

- Define crouching as a posture, not an emotion.
- Compare relaxed loaf, hunting crouch, fear/freeze, defensive crouch, pain/hunch and toileting strain.
- Use muscle tension, weight distribution, face, tail, trigger and transition.
- Give a no-contact response to fear and defensive tension.
- Explain that pain cannot be diagnosed from posture alone.
- Make repeated litter-box attempts with little or no urine an emergency, especially in male cats.
- Include breathing difficulty and open-mouth breathing as urgent signs.
- Explain photo/video limitations and what to record for a veterinarian.
