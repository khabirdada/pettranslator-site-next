# Keyword brief: cat non-recognition aggression

## Search intent

Urgent informational and recovery intent. A familiar cat returns from a vet, groomer or absence and a housemate suddenly hisses, avoids or attacks. The reader wants to know why, what to do now, how long separation should last and how to prevent recurrence.

## Duplication audit

`/blog/why-is-my-cat-acting-weird` briefly names post-vet non-recognition. `/blog/cat-redirected-aggression` includes it only as a differential. `/blog/introduce-new-cat-to-resident-cat` gives a general introduction protocol. No article owns the altered-scent/changed-behavior trigger, the homecoming decision, anesthesia and pain precautions, or prevention for repeat veterinary visits.

Create a dedicated page. Keep redirected aggression distinct because its defining sequence is arousal by an inaccessible trigger followed by an attack on a nearby target.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from Cornell, Anti-Cruelty, Catster, veterinary clinics and trainer sites focus on unfamiliar clinic scent. AAFP/ISFM handling guidance also considers the returning cat's behavior and recommends observing household response before reunion. Broader feline behavior guidance supports slow reintroduction and medical evaluation where pain or illness may contribute.

The content needs to avoid presenting smell as the only mechanism. Clinic odors, fear arousal, altered gait or appearance, pain, medication, anesthesia and time apart can combine. It should also avoid baths, forced scent rubbing and close carrier exposure as universal fixes.

## Content opportunity

- Give an immediate homecoming decision based on both cats' body language.
- Explain multiple contributors rather than a single “identity scent” story.
- Separate cats safely at the first hiss, stare, block or chase.
- Use behavior-based recovery, not a fixed number of hours.
- Provide scent exchange without forcing cloth onto either cat.
- Build a staged door, barrier and supervised reintroduction.
- Cover anesthesia confinement and procedure-specific veterinary instructions.
- Plan future visits with familiar bedding, predictable carrier work and a prepared recovery room.
- Include fight-injury and medication red flags.
