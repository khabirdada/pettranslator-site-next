# Keyword brief: cat skin twitching when petted

## Search intent

Informational with medical triage. The reader sees the skin along a cat's back ripple during or after petting and wants to know whether it is normal sensitivity, overstimulation, itch, pain or feline hyperesthesia syndrome.

## Duplication audit

`/blog/cat-overstimulation-signs` owns the petting-to-tail-flick-to-bite sequence and briefly flags skin rippling. `/blog/why-is-my-cat-acting-weird` mentions hyperesthesia among sudden behavior changes. No page owns skin twitching as a symptom, compares dermatologic, musculoskeletal, neurologic and interaction contexts, or explains why feline hyperesthesia is a syndrome considered after other causes.

Create a dedicated medical-differential page. Link to the overstimulation page for interaction management rather than repeating its full consent protocol.

## Current result-page patterns

Current results from Cornell, PetMD, Veterinary Partner and general pet sites heavily feature feline hyperesthesia syndrome. Cornell stresses evaluation for pain and skin problems such as parasites, allergy and fungal infection. Reviews and case material describe episodic skin rippling with vocalization, pupil dilation, frantic running, tail chasing, excessive grooming or self-trauma. Evidence does not support diagnosing FHS from one isolated ripple.

## Content opportunity

- Define the visible event without diagnosing it.
- Separate a brief isolated cutaneous reflex from a repeated episode.
- Compare petting overstimulation, fleas/allergy/skin disease, spinal or tail-base pain and FHS-like clusters.
- Tell the reader to stop touch rather than repeatedly test the area.
- Provide a useful video and episode log for the veterinarian.
- Explain diagnostic exclusion and possible dermatology, pain and neurology workup.
- Include self-injury, seizure-like activity, toxin and severe-pain urgent flags.
- Avoid unsupported home supplements or medication advice.
