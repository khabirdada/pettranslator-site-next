# Keyword brief: cat play aggression

## Decision

Publish a dedicated guide at `/blog/cat-play-aggression`.

The existing `/blog/cats-playing-or-fighting` page answers an inter-cat diagnostic question. `/blog/cat-love-bites` and `/blog/cat-petting-aggression` cover biting during touch. This keyword has a separate, mostly human-directed intent: stalking ankles, ambushing feet, grabbing hands, biting and scratching during predatory play, and choosing safer outlets.

## Search intent

- Identify whether biting and ambushing are play aggression.
- Stop attacks on hands, feet and ankles safely.
- Understand why kittens and young cats do it.
- Choose toys, routines and environmental changes that help.
- Know when the behavior is not play and needs veterinary help.

## Primary keyword

`cat play aggression`

## Supporting language

- play aggression in cats
- cat attacks ankles
- cat bites during play
- kitten attacks hands and feet
- stop cat play biting
- predatory play in cats
- aggressive cat play

## Differentiation

The page should:

- Center interactions between a cat and a person, not wrestling between cats.
- Separate play aggression from petting-induced, fear-based and redirected aggression.
- Explain that the motor pattern is predatory play and that the target is inappropriate.
- Avoid implying that the cat is malicious, dominant or in need of punishment.
- Avoid rigid daily play prescriptions unsupported by individual context.

## Recommended structure

1. Fast identification checklist.
2. Why the behavior happens.
3. Play aggression versus other causes.
4. What to do during an ambush.
5. A prevention plan using distance toys, predictable outlets and food enrichment.
6. Common mistakes.
7. Veterinary and human medical safety thresholds.

## Source notes

- AAFP/ISFM environmental-needs guidance supports opportunities for predatory play, wand toys, capture, post-play rewards, food puzzles, toy rotation and keeping hands and feet out of play.
- The peer-reviewed indoor-cat enrichment review explicitly links hand-and-foot play with owner-directed play aggression and recommends toys that preserve distance.
- Merck Veterinary Manual identifies play aggression alongside biting, chasing and play fighting.
- Veterinary behavior guidance emphasizes identifying triggers, enrichment, relationship repair and behavior modification rather than punishment.
