## Query evidence used

### Pet translator apps

Visible queries included `do pet translators work` (31 impressions, position 7.5), `do dog translator apps actually work` (22, 7.8), `do dog translators work` (21, 9.6), `dog translator` (12, 3.8), and several “real” or “really work” variants. The new title and description answer that skepticism directly without promising literal translation.

### Cat skin rippling during petting

Visible queries included `cat skin ripples when pet` (3 impressions, position 10), `why do cats skin twitch when you pet them` (2, 9), `cat back ripples when pet` (1, 1), and close singular variants. The new title now mirrors the highest-intent natural-language question.

### Cat overstimulation

Search Console exposed `overstimulated cat`, `overstimulation in cats`, `cat overstimulation`, `cat overstimulated bite`, and source-led queries around petting aggression. The page keeps petting aggression in the body but moves the broader phrase readers use into the title.

### Cat water play, hiding, stiff dogs and ground sniffing

Visible supporting queries included `cat playing with water`, `cat hiding after moving`, `dog stiff body language`, `dog sniffing ground`, and `dog sniffing the ground`. Titles that already matched those intents were preserved unless the snippet could become clearer or safer.

## Current-result comparison

Current English results were checked on 2026-09-01. Representative result types and domains included:

- Translator apps: an exact-intent explainer on `translate.dog` plus video results. The useful gap is an explicit line between entertainment captions, audio classification and cautious visual observation.
- Night fighting: PetMD, PetsRadar, VCA, WebMD and forum discussions. The useful gap is a fast play-versus-conflict test, resource access, safe separation and what to do when the pattern starts suddenly.
- Skin rippling: Dial A Vet, BC SPCA, WebMD, Cats Protection material and community questions. The useful gap is avoiding an automatic hyperesthesia diagnosis while showing the sign cluster that warrants veterinary care.
- Sleep barking: PetMD and Dogster. The useful gap is a concise loose-REM-versus-rigid-seizure comparison and a practical filming protocol.
- Overstimulated cats: PetMD, Catster, Cats.com and humane-society material. The useful gap is the early warning sequence and a pause-based choice method before a bite.
- Stiffness and sniffing: PetMD, Tufts, RSPCA, AKC and specialist explainers. The useful gap is distinguishing a single pose from a change over time, then giving a low-risk response.

Competitor pages established intent and expected coverage only. Medical and behavior claims remain tied to the authoritative sources already cited inside each article.

## Verification and next measurement

Use 2026-09-01 as the change boundary. Recheck after enough post-change impressions accumulate, ideally at 14 days for an early read and again at 28 days. Compare page and query CTR at similar positions; do not treat a ranking shift as a snippet win. Keep signups and paid conversions beside Search Console clicks so a higher CTR that attracts the wrong audience is not counted as success.
